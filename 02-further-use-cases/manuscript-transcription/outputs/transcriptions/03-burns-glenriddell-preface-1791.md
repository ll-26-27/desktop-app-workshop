# 03-burns-glenriddell-preface-1791

## About this page

Robert Burns's preface to the first volume of the Glenriddell Manuscript, the
collection of his poems he copied for Robert Riddell of Glenriddell. Signed and
dated 27 April 1791. English prose in Burns's own hand, a fluent late
18th-century round hand. The image is from the 1914 photographic facsimile.

**Condition:** clear. A few ink blots (line 5, *come*; line 9, *I*; line 15,
*&*; line 16, after *Life*). The facsimile reproduces them as dark marks.

**Recognized text:** I know of this preface but do not have its wording by
heart. The transcription is from the image.

## Marginalia and later additions

1. **Top center: "ATHENÆUM / LIVERPOOL."** A printed ownership stamp, not in
   Burns's hand. It records a later owner of the manuscript volume. Confident.
2. **Line 7, after *give*:** a small vertical mark, possibly a caret (^)
   marking an insertion. No inserted word is visible above the line or in the
   margin. [?caret]
3. **Left margin, beside line 6:** a short curved stroke before *taken*. It may
   be a pen slip or a mark keyed to the caret in line 7.
4. **Bottom center, below line 24:** a small mark, possibly a page number or
   a mark from the facsimile. [illegible]

## Reading text

As this Collection almost wholly consists of pieces local or unfinished,
fragments the effusion of a poetical moment & bagatelles strung in rhyme simply
pour passer le temps, the Author trusts that nobody into whose hands it may
come will without his permission give or allow to be taken, copies of any thing
here contained; much less to give to the world at large, what he never meant
should see the light. At the Gentleman's request, whose from this time it shall
be, the Collection was made; and to him, & I will add, to his amiable Lady, it
is presented, as a sincere though small tribute of gratitude for the many many
happy hours the Author has spent under their roof. There, what Poverty even
though accompanied with Genius must seldom expect to meet with at the tables &
in the circles of Fashionable Life, his welcome has ever been, the cordiality of
Kindness, & the warmth of Friendship. As from the situation in which it is now
placed, this M.S.S. may be preserved, & this Preface read, when the hand that
now writes & the heart that now dictates it may be mouldering in the dust; let
these be regarded as the genuine sentiments of a man who seldom flattered any,
& never those he loved.

27th April 1791. Robt Burns

*pour passer le temps*: French, "to pass the time." Burns's spelling and
punctuation are kept; only the long s is modernized.

## Uncertainties

1. Line 8, *[?whoſe]*: the word at the end of the line is cramped against the
   edge; the long s is likely but not certain.
2. Line 7, the mark after *give*: a caret with no visible insertion, or an
   accidental stroke. The original manuscript would settle whether a word such
   as *them* was added and has faded.
3. Line 3, *paſser*, line 5, *permiſsion*, line 6, *leſs*, line 17, *Kindneſs*:
   long s in the double-s pairs, as was usual in this period. Other *s* forms
   may also be long s where the hand is less distinct.
4. Marginalia 3 and 4: unread.
