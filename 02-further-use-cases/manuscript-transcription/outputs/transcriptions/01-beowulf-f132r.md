# 01-beowulf-f132r

## About this page

The opening page of *Beowulf*, British Library, Cotton MS Vitellius A XV,
f. 132r. Old English (West Saxon), written in insular minuscule by the first of
the manuscript's two scribes, around 975–1025. The first line is in display
capitals after a large initial *H*.

**Condition:** the right edge and the lower right corner are damaged; the
manuscript was scorched in the Cotton Library fire of 1731, and letters are lost
at the ends of several lines. A stain in the lower middle of the page makes
line 18 hard to read.

**Recognized text:** yes. This is one of the best-known pages in English
literature and its text is in my training data. The readings below follow the
visible letters. Where the page is damaged and a reading comes from knowing the
poem, it is marked `[supplied: …]`.

## Marginalia and later additions

1. **Top right, beside line 2: "129"** in a later hand, in ink. A folio number
   from an earlier foliation of the volume; the current British Library folio
   number is 132. Confident.
2. **Above line 6, over *egsode*:** a short word in a small, later cursive hand.
   I cannot read it at this resolution. It may be a gloss or a correction.
   [illegible]
3. **Bottom margin:** faint strokes in a later hand, possibly pen trials or
   letters. [illegible]
4. **Left edge, bottom:** a small ink mark, probably not a letter.

## Reading text

Line breaks follow the verse, not the page. Abbreviations are expanded
(ꝥ → þæt, ū → um).

> Hwæt, we Gar-Dena in geardagum,
> þeodcyninga þrym gefrunon,
> hu ða æþelingas ellen fremedon.
> Oft Scyld Scefing sceaþena þreatum,
> monegum mægþum meodosetla ofteah,
> egsode eorl, syððan ærest wearð
> feasceaft funden; he þæs frofre gebad,
> weox under wolcnum, weorðmyndum þah,
> oð þæt him æghwylc þara ymbsittendra
> ofer hronrade hyran scolde,
> gomban gyldan. Þæt wæs god cyning.
> Ðæm eafera wæs æfter cenned
> geong in geardum, þone God sende
> folce to frofre; fyrenðearfe ongeat
> þæt hie ær drugon aldorlease
> lange hwile. Him þæs Liffrea,
> wuldres Wealdend, woroldare forgeaf;
> Beowulf wæs breme (blæd wide sprang),
> Scyldes eafera Scedelandum in.
> Swa sceal geong guma gode gewyrcean,
> fromum feohgiftum on fæder …

The reading text uses *w* for wynn (ƿ), as most editions do.

## Translation

Listen! We have heard of the glory of the kings of the Spear-Danes in days of
old, how those princes did deeds of courage. Often Scyld Scefing took the mead
benches away from troops of enemies, from many peoples; he terrified the
warriors, after he was first found destitute. He lived to see consolation for
that: he grew under the heavens and prospered in honors, until each of the
neighboring peoples across the whale-road had to obey him and pay tribute. That
was a good king. To him a son was born afterward, young in the courts, whom God
sent as a comfort to the people; He had seen the dire distress they had
suffered before, without a lord, for a long time. For this the Lord of life,
the Ruler of glory, gave him honor in the world. Beowulf was famous (his renown
spread widely), the heir of Scyld in the Scandinavian lands. So should a young
man bring about good by generous gifts in his father's [keeping] …

## Uncertainties

1. Line 3, *elle[n]*: final letter lost at the burnt edge; supplied from the
   poem.
2. Line 4, *sceaþe[na]*: ending lost at the edge; supplied.
3. Line 5, *setl[a]*: final letter lost; supplied.
4. Line 6, *ƿear[ð]*: final letter lost; supplied.
5. Line 7, *geba[d]*: final letter lost; supplied.
6. Line 14, *aldo[…2…]ase*: about two letters are unreadable in the damaged
   area; the reading text's *aldorlease* comes from knowing the poem, not from
   the image.
7. Line 15, *ƿealden[d]*: final letter at the edge; supplied.
8. Line 18, *[geong g]uma*: several letters are illegible under the stain;
   supplied.
9. Line 19, *fæde[r]*: final letter lost; supplied.
10. Line 16, *beoƿulf*: the manuscript clearly reads *beowulf* here. Many
    editors emend it to *Beow*, since this is Scyld's son, not the poem's hero.
    The transcription keeps what the page says.
11. Line 6, *eorl*: the manuscript reads *eorl*; some editions emend to
    *eorlas*.
12. Marginalia 2 and 3: unread. A higher-resolution image would settle them.
