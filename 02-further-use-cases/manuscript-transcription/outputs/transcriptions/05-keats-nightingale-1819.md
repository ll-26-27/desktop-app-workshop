# 05-keats-nightingale-1819

## About this page

A holograph of John Keats's "Ode to a Nightingale" (headed "Ode to the
Nightingale"), May 1819. The page gives the first two and a half stanzas, with
deletions and replacement words written above the line.

**Condition:** legible but small (461 × 668 pixels) and high in contrast. Words
struck through, and the small words written above them, are the hardest part.

**Recognized text:** yes. The published poem is in my training data. The
draft's deletions and first readings are transcribed from the image; where a
struck word is read partly from knowing the poem, it is marked `[?…]`.

## Reading text

> **Ode to the Nightingale**
>
> My heart aches, and a drowsy numbness pains
> My sense, as though of hemlock I had drunk,
> Or emptied some dull opiate to the drains
> One minute past, and Lethe-wards had sunk:
> 'Tis not through envy of thy happy lot,
> But being too happy in thine happiness,
> That thou, light-winged dryad of the trees,
> In some melodious plot
> Of beechen green, and shadows numberless,
> Singest of summer in full-throated ease.
>
> O for a draught of vintage that has been
> Cooling a long age in the deep-delved earth,
> Tasting of Flora and the country green,
> Dance, and provencal song, and sunburnt mirth!
> O for a Beaker full of the warm South,
> Full of the true and blushful Hippocrene,
> With cluster'd bubbles winking at the brim,
> And purple stained mouth;
> That I might drink, and leave the world unseen,
> And with thee fade away into forest dim:
>
> Fade far away, dissolve, and quite forget
> What thou among the leaves hast never known,
> The weariness, the fever, and the fret
> Here, where men sit and hear each other groan;
> Where palsy shakes a few sad, last, grey hairs,
> Where youth grows pale, and spectre-thin, and dies;

Punctuation at line ends is light in the draft; the reading text adds only what
is needed to read it and follows the draft's words.

## Uncertainties

1. Line 2: *painful* struck and *drowsy* written above; *falls* struck and
   *pains* written above. Both replacements are small but clear enough.
2. Line 8, *[?That]*: the first word is blotted or overwritten.
3. Line 13: *long* is written above between *a* and *age*; whether *an* was
   corrected to *a* is not clear in this image.
4. Line 20, *[?leave]*: the word is overwritten or heavily inked.
5. Line 21, *~~the~~*: a short struck word before *forest*; probable.
6. Line 27: the most revised line on the page. A struck word after *Where*,
   a small *a* above, *spectre* above *thin*, a struck *and*, and *and dies*
   written below the line. The order of these changes cannot be settled from
   this image.
7. Provenance: this image's source describes it only as a holograph copy from
   May 1819. The page's location and whether this is the first draft should be
   checked before citing it.
