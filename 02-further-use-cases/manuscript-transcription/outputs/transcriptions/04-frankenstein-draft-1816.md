# 04-frankenstein-draft-1816

## About this page

A page of Mary Shelley's draft of *Frankenstein*, from the notebooks in the
Abinger papers at the Bodleian Library, written 1816–17. The draft heading
reads "Chapter 7th"; in the 1818 edition this passage opens chapter 5 ("It was
on a dreary night of November"). The main text is in Mary Shelley's hand.
Several revisions, between the lines and in the left margin, look like they are
in a second hand and a darker ink. Percy Shelley is known to have revised this
draft, but I cannot confirm from this image which revisions are his.

**Condition:** legible, but the image is small (500 × 753 pixels), so heavily
struck words and small interlinear additions are hard to read.

**Recognized text:** yes. The published text of this passage is in my training
data. The draft differs from it in many places, and the transcription follows
the draft. Where a struck or faint word is read partly from knowing the
passage, it is marked `[?…]`.

## Marginalia and later additions

1. **Top right: "21"** and **a circled "95"**, in later hands. Probably a folio
   number and a page or sequence number added by later owners or cataloguers.
   Confident about the numbers, not their purpose.
2. **Left margin, lines 23–26:** the three additions listed above. They are in
   a darker ink and a more angular hand than the main text, and they supply the
   wording of the 1818 edition ("beautiful," "yellow skin," "hair of a lustrous
   black"). They may be Percy Shelley's. [?second hand]
3. **Interlinear, line 6: "that I might"**, and **line 23: "Beautiful"**, look
   like the same darker ink. [?second hand]
4. **Interlinear, line 3:** "the frame on whic[h]" is written above *my man
   compleated* and then itself struck through. Hard to read. [?]

## Reading text

Chapter 7th

It was on a dreary night of November that I beheld … with an anxiety that
almost amounted to agony I collected the instruments of life around me that I
might infuse a spark of being into the lifeless thing that lay at my feet. It
was already one in the morning, the rain pattered dismally against the window
panes, & my candle was nearly burnt out, when by the glimmer of the half
extinguished light I saw the dull yellow eye of the creature open. It breathed
hard, and a convulsive motion agitated its limbs.

How can I describe my emotion at this catastrophe, or how delineate the wretch
whom with such infinite pains and care I had endeavoured to form. His limbs
were in proportion and I had selected his features as beautiful! Beautiful,
Great God! His yellow skin scarcely covered the work of muscles and arteries
beneath; his hair was of a lustrous black & flowing and his teeth of a pearly
whiteness but these luxuriances only formed a more horrid contrast with his
watry eyes that seemed almost of the same colour as the dun white sockets in
which they were set,

The reading text follows the page's latest layer. In line 3, both the original
wording and its replacement are struck, so the object of *beheld* is left open
(…). The 1818 edition reads "the accomplishment of my toils."

## Uncertainties

1. Line 3: the interlinear "the frame on whic[h]" is a probable reading of a
   small, struck phrase; the word *compleated* (Mary Shelley's spelling) is
   clear. The Shelley-Godwin Archive transcription would settle what was
   written and struck here, and in what order.
2. Line 5, *[?the]*: a short word between *collected* and *instruments* is
   crowded; it may be struck.
3. Line 6, *~~and endeavoured to~~*: the struck words are read partly from
   letter shapes and partly from sense. [?]
4. Line 17, *~~But how~~*: struck; probable.
5. Line 23: the heavily blotted word in the main line is read as *handsome*
   from the matching word in the margin.
6. Line 27, *these*: there may be a line through it; if so it is struck and
   then kept in the published text.
7. Marginalia 2 and 3: attribution to a second hand is based only on ink and
   letter shapes in a small image. The Shelley-Godwin Archive attributes each
   revision to Mary or Percy Shelley.
