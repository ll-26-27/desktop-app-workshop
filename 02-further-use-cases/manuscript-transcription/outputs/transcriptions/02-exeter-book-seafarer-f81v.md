## Translation

I can tell a true story about myself, speak of my journeys: how in days of toil
I often suffered times of hardship, have endured bitter sorrow of heart, have
known many halls of care aboard ship, the terrible rolling of the waves, where
the anxious night watch often held me at the ship's prow as it beat along the
cliffs. My feet were pinched with cold, bound by frost in cold fetters, where
cares sighed hot about my heart; hunger tore from within the mind of the
sea-weary man. The man who fares most pleasantly on land does not know how I,
wretched and sorrowful, spent the winter on the ice-cold sea on the paths of
exile, cut off from kinsmen, hung about with icicles; hail flew in showers.
There I heard nothing but the roaring sea, the ice-cold wave. At times I took
the song of the swan as my entertainment, the cry of the gannet and the call of
the curlew in place of the laughter of men, the singing gull in place of mead.
Storms beat the stone cliffs there, where the tern answered them,
icy-feathered; very often the eagle screamed around, its feathers wet with
spray; no protecting kinsman could comfort the desolate spirit. And so the man
who has known the joy of life in towns, and few hard journeys, proud and
flushed with wine, little believes how I, weary, often had to stay out on the
sea-road. The shadow of night grew dark, it snowed from the north, frost bound
the ground, hail fell on the earth, the coldest of grain. And so the thoughts of
my heart now press on me, that I … [continues on the next page]

2. Line 10, *is [?c]aldne*: the letter before *aldne* is unclear. Editions
   print *iscealdne*; the image looks closer to *is caldne* (compare line 12).
3. Line 14, *singen / de*: the letters at the line end are crowded; there may be
   an abbreviation mark.
4. Line 17, *[?nænig]*: could be *nænig* or *ne ænig*. Editions differ; the
   image is too small to settle it.
5. Line 20, *[?sceolde]*: could be *scolde*; the letter after *sc* is unclear.
6. Throughout: at this resolution, *ƿ* and *p* are hard to tell apart. Every
