# Manuscript page images

Five single-page images, from the tenth century to the nineteenth. All are
public domain and were downloaded from Wikimedia Commons in September 2026.
Filenames begin with a number so the pages sort in date order.

| file | what it is | date | why it is here |
|---|---|---|---|
| `01-beowulf-f132r.jpg` | *Beowulf*, opening page. British Library, Cotton MS Vitellius A XV, f. 132r | c. 975–1025 | Old English; the letter wynn (ƿ), which looks like *p*; edges lost to fire damage; a later folio number and interlinear note |
| `02-exeter-book-seafarer-f81v.jpg` | *The Seafarer*, opening page. Exeter Cathedral Library, the Exeter Book, f. 81v | 10th century | Old English in a different hand; lower resolution; abbreviations (the Tironian *et*, ⁊) |
| `03-burns-glenriddell-preface-1791.jpg` | Robert Burns, preface to the Glenriddell Manuscript, vol. 1, from the 1914 facsimile | 27 April 1791 | 18th-century hand; long s (ſ); ampersands and superscript abbreviations; a later library stamp |
| `04-frankenstein-draft-1816.jpg` | Mary Shelley, draft of *Frankenstein*, opening of the chapter that became chapter 5 in 1818. Bodleian Library, Abinger papers | 1816–17 | deletions, interlinear revisions, and additions in the left margin |
| `05-keats-nightingale-1819.jpg` | John Keats, "Ode to a Nightingale," holograph | May 1819 | a poet's draft: struck words with replacements written above |

The last two images are small (about 500 px wide). They are included because
low resolution is a real condition of archival work, and because it shows where
a model starts to guess.

## Editions to check against

Each text has a standard edition or digital transcription. Use them to check
the outputs, not as input to the model:

- *Beowulf*: Klaeber's *Beowulf*, 4th ed. (Fulk, Bjork, and Niles, 2008), or the
  *Electronic Beowulf* (Kiernan)
- *The Seafarer*: Krapp and Dobbie, *The Exeter Book* (Anglo-Saxon Poetic
  Records III, 1936)
- Burns: the transcription printed with the 1914 Glenriddell facsimile
- *Frankenstein*: the Shelley-Godwin Archive, which transcribes the draft
  notebooks and attributes each revision to Mary or Percy Shelley
- Keats: Jack Stillinger, ed., *The Poems of John Keats* (1978)
